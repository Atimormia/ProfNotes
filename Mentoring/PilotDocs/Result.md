# HR Program Review: Mentoring Framework Pilot, Season 1

**Scope:** Program-level review of the framework itself, using this pilot's single pairing (Alex Chen / Marcus Webb) as the pilot's only data point. This document evaluates whether the *framework* is worth adopting company-wide, not primarily whether this specific pairing succeeded, though the two are naturally connected in a one-pairing pilot.
**Author:** HR Program Coordinator, with input from Priya Fernandes and David Okafor.
**Access:** HR and Managers; presented to Mentors and future participants in a non-attributable, program-level form.

---

## Expectations, Stated Before the Pilot Began

Before Season 1 started, HR set out what this pilot needed to demonstrate to justify rolling the framework out beyond one pairing:

1. **Visibility.** Produce documented, comparable evidence of a Mentee's growth over a season, something the company's prior informal mentoring (pairing documented, nothing else) never generated.
2. **Diagnostic accuracy.** Show that a gap identified at the start of a season can be tracked, and if the initial read was wrong or incomplete, that the framework catches and corrects it rather than reporting false progress.
3. **Mentor development, not just Mentee development.** Test whether the framework's Mentor Growth toolkit produces real, visible change in a Mentor's own behavior over a season, since the program's stated goals include leadership development, not only skill transfer.
4. **Honest reporting of a partial result.** Confirm the framework can produce a credible report when the outcome is mixed, not only when everything closes cleanly, since a framework that only looks good in success stories isn't trustworthy for a company-wide rollout.
5. **A read on time investment.** Establish whether a 3-month pilot season is long enough to produce a meaningful result at all, for either a Mentee's technical gap or a Mentor's own developmental goal.
6. **Documentation readiness.** Test whether Mentors and Mentees, with no prior experience producing this kind of structured educational record, can actually meet Guide 04's minimal content bar without extensive HR intervention, since a framework whose documentation only works with heavy hand-holding doesn't scale past a pilot.

## Outcome, Measured Against Each Expectation

**1. Visibility — met.** For the first time, HR has a complete, dated trail from Baseline through three Module Outcome Records to a Season Summary and Manager Comparison Record. Priya's independent confirmation of reduced review cycle time, cross-referenced against the Mentee's and Mentor's own accounts, is exactly the kind of multi-source evidence the prior informal program never produced.

**2. Diagnostic accuracy — met, and demonstrated directly.** The Baseline's gap classification was revised at the Season Summary once evidence accumulated (a compound practice-and-judgment gap narrowed into a closed practice gap and a more precisely defined, still-open judgment gap specific to pressure conditions). This is not a failure of the initial diagnosis; it is the framework's Season Synthesis mechanism working as designed, catching a classification that needed sharpening rather than letting an imprecise label stand for the whole season.

**3. Mentor development — partially met.** The Mentor Growth toolkit's Showcasing Instead of Teaching pattern was correctly surfaced early (Module 1) through the separate-before-reconciled outcome record mechanism, and Marcus's behavior measurably shifted afterward (Module 2's record shows deliberate, self-reported effort and a visible reduction in the pattern). However, the broader organizational goal set for Marcus, growth toward an architecture-track role, was honestly assessed as not reached within one season. This is recorded as a finding about the pilot's design, not as a failure by Marcus or a flaw in the toolkit.

**4. Honest reporting of a partial result — met.** Both the Season Summary and this review report the judgment gap's reappearance under real pressure plainly, without reframing it as a success or burying it. The framework's structure (separate independent drafts, a defined closure definition to measure against) made this possible; neither party had to editorialize the result into looking better than the evidence supported.

**5. Time investment — mixed, and this pilot surfaced two distinct findings rather than one.** First: three months proved sufficient to close a well-scoped technical practice gap, including under real, unplanned pressure, but insufficient to fully close a judgment gap under pressure specifically, which appears to need sustained exposure over more than one season regardless of season length. Second, and distinct from the first: Marcus's architecture-track goal did not advance meaningfully, but this pilot cannot actually distinguish whether that is because one season is too short, or because no second season was ever committed to in the first place, since the two were never tested separately. Future seasons should not assume this pilot proved "three months is too short for role-track growth"; it only proved that one uncommitted season wasn't enough, which is a weaker and different claim.

**6. Documentation readiness — mixed, with a clear improvement trend.** Module 1's outcome record required direct HR follow-up after an initial draft that didn't meet the minimal content bar. Module 2 showed substantial improvement but surfaced a specific, recurring confusion between Definition of Done and closure definition (see Guide 04) that briefly produced an inaccurate reconciled summary before HR intake caught it. By Module 3, both parties produced complete, largely self-sufficient records. The trend is encouraging, but a single pairing's learning curve isn't yet evidence the framework's documentation is intuitive without support; see recommendations below.

## Conclusion and Recommendations

**Recommend adopting the framework company-wide**, on the strength of the visibility, diagnostic accuracy, and honest-reporting results above, all of which represent a clear improvement over the prior informal program regardless of this pairing's mixed technical outcome.

**Two structural adjustments recommended for future seasons**, based directly on this pilot's evidence rather than on the framework's original design assumptions:

* Where a Mentor's own goal involves role-track growth (architecture, leadership, or similar), track that goal at a season-over-season grain from the outset, rather than expecting a single 3-month pilot to show meaningful movement. This should be stated explicitly in the Individual Goals and Baseline Document going forward, so a future Mentor and their manager aren't measuring against an implicit timeline the framework never actually promised.
* Where a diagnosed gap has both a practice component and a judgment component, plan for the possibility that they close at different rates, and consider a season boundary drawn around the practice component alone, with the judgment component explicitly carried forward, rather than treating a single season as needing to close everything identified at Baseline.

**Library note:** Module 1 (naive baseline and benchmarking) and Module 2 (SoA rewrite with Skeleton-Meat partitioning) are recommended for the shared library pending use with a second Mentee, per Guide 02's promotion criteria. Module 3 is not recommended for promotion in its current, reduced form, since its scope reduction was pilot-specific and a full-scope version has not yet been tested.
