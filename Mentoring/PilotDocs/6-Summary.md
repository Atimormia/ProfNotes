# Season Summary

**Pairing:** Alex Chen (Mentee) and Marcus Webb (Mentor)
**Season:** Pilot Season 1, Weeks 1–12
**Access:** Mentor and Mentee; shared with Priya Fernandes and HR

## Season Synthesis

Reading the three Module Outcome Records as a trajectory rather than as three separate results: the primary practice gap identified at Baseline, modern C++ ownership and memory mechanics, closed cleanly and held up under real, unplanned time pressure in Month 3, which is a stronger result than closing under planned conditions alone would have been.

The secondary judgment gap, knowing when interface-based abstraction is worth its structural cost in C++, followed a different curve. It narrowed visibly under Module 2's deliberate, moderate pressure, then reappeared under Module 3's real, compressed timeline. This is not read as the gap failing to close; it is read as evidence that the original Baseline classification undersold how much harder a judgment gap is to close than a practice gap, exactly the distinction Guide 01 draws between the two.

**Revised Classification:** What was recorded at Baseline as a single, compound gap (practice plus emerging judgment) is now understood more precisely: the practice component has closed. The judgment component should be tracked in any future season not as "abstraction-cost judgment" generally, but specifically as "abstraction-cost judgment under real deadline pressure," since the evidence shows Alex applies the judgment correctly when there is time to think and reverts under compression. This is the exact pattern Guide 01's own worked example anticipates: an original classification that needs sharpening once a season's evidence accumulates.

## Results Against Original Goals

Alex's personal goal ("stop feeling like I'm translating from C# in my head") is substantially met for ownership and memory mechanics specifically, and openly not yet met for structural judgment under pressure. Priya's project-specific goal (reduced review cycle time) shows measurable improvement, independently confirmed by peer input in the Module 3 record, even accounting for the Month 3 reversion.

## What Worked

* Sequencing the practice gap before the judgment gap gave Alex a stable technical foundation before being asked to make harder structural calls.
* The Skeleton-Meat format in Module 2 directly addressed the habit flagged at Baseline (re-litigating structure) by removing the interface-design decision from Alex's scope for one round.
* Separate-before-reconciled outcome records surfaced the Mentor-side teaching pattern (Guide 06) early, in Module 1, where both parties named it independently before discussing it, rather than it going unaddressed for the whole season.
* The documentation discipline itself improved visibly module over module, from a two-sentence first draft needing direct HR follow-up in Module 1 to a substantially complete, largely self-sufficient record by Module 3. Neither party had used this kind of structured reflective documentation before this season.

## What Didn't, and Why

* Three months was not enough time for the judgment gap to become automatic under pressure, even though it closed reliably under calm conditions. This appears to be a property of judgment gaps generally (see Guide 01's own worked example) rather than something specific to this pairing.
* The Mentor's own development goal (see Manager Comparison Record) was only partially advanced. This was expected from the outset per the Mentoring Strategy Document, which named architecture-track growth as a multi-season goal; the limiting factor is that this pilot was scoped as a single season with no committed continuation, not that one season of effort was insufficient in some open-ended way. Whether a second season happens at all is a decision outside this document's scope (see HR Program Review).

## Plans for Next Season

If continued: a second season should track the judgment gap specifically under pressure conditions rather than treating it as closed, and should consider whether Marcus continuing as Mentor (now with one season of practice at Do Together review) or a rematch better serves both the Mentee's continued growth and the Mentor's own architecture-track goal, which may benefit from mentoring a second Mentee to build a genuine track record rather than repeating with the same pairing.
