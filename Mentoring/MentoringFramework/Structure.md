# Mentoring Program Structure

# Introduce Mentoring Program

## Define Session Periods

Set up regular mentoring periods each year. Align these with broader education sessions to avoid conflicts with vacations and project deadlines. Options include:

* **Three 3-Month Sessions per Year**: More frequent opportunities for mentoring, ideal for shorter-term goals or a larger pool of participants.  
* **Two 4-Month Sessions per Year**: Longer sessions allowing more in-depth focus and development.  
* **Custom Periods**: Flexibly aligned with specific project releases and seasonal workloads to fit team schedules and ensure availability.

## Prepare Program Platform

Establish a **shared digital workspace** where participants can access orientation materials, mentorship resources, templates, and goal-setting tools. The platform should include:

* **A progress tracking board** to keep mentors and mentees aligned on goals, document achievements, and track milestones.  
* **Communication channels** for mentors, mentees, and program coordinators, ensuring an accessible place for discussions, updates, and feedback.

## Program Announcement

Communicate session start dates, goals, and program benefits through email, the company intranet, and team meetings. Use recurring reminders to build awareness. This includes:

* **Program Goals**: Clarify the purpose of mentoring, emphasizing benefits like skill development, knowledge sharing, and career growth.  
* **Expectations**: Outline the roles and responsibilities for mentors, mentees, and managers, detailing time commitments and how progress will be assessed.  
* **Benefits**: Highlight personal and professional rewards, such as enhanced skills, networking, and increased job satisfaction.

**Result**: This introduction ensures everyone in the company is aware of the program, its schedule, and its purpose. Building this awareness will foster anticipation, encourage active participation, and promote a culture of continuous learning and mutual support.

# Phase 0\. Prepare for Mentoring Session

## Mentoring Session Announcement

* **Program Overview**: Share the mentoring concept, program structure and dates, and specific goals across the company, ensuring clarity on expectations for all participants.  
* **Call for Participants**: Issue a call for mentors and mentees, restating the program’s goals, structure, and expectations.   
* **Dedicated Time for Mentoring**: Clarify how much working time can be allocated for mentoring or if sessions will require a mix of working and personal time. Provide flexibility where possible to support diverse schedules.  
* **Motivation and Incentives**: Specify material (e.g., potential bonuses, rewards) and non-material (e.g., recognition, career advancement opportunities) incentives to motivate participation and show program value.

## Identifying Candidates

1. **Manager-Employee Meetings**: Managers meet with their team members to review personal and project-specific development goals, identifying who would benefit most from mentoring. HR supports this process by preparing an overview of participants’ goals and development plans to provide additional context and alignment.  
2. **HR Outreach**: HR can initiate a call for mentors and mentees through internal announcements, open enrollment forms, or direct invitations.  
3. **Optional Matching Process**: Set up a formal matching process if desired, involving forms or recent employee review documentation. Alternatively, allow mentors and mentees to find their own partners for a more organic pairing process

## Mentoring Session Kickoff

* **Session Start Confirmation**: Announce the official start of the mentoring session, confirming mentor-mentee pairs to foster transparency and engagement.

**Result**: Mentor-mentee pairs are identified, matched, and announced, setting a clear foundation for the mentoring program and ensuring both participants and managers are aligned on expectations and goals.

# Phase 1\. Goal setup

## Onboarding and Mentor Preparation

* **Participant Onboarding**: Conduct an onboarding session covering roles, available toolkits, and communication protocols for the mentoring program. Ensure participants know where to access resources and whom to contact for support.  
* **Optional Mentor Training**: Provide optional workshops or training sessions for mentors, focusing on strategies for effective mentoring. Topics can include:  
  * Goal-setting techniques  
  * Structuring mentoring sessions  
  * Psychological tools for effective feedback  
  * Best practices for ongoing mentor-mentee evaluations and adjustments

## Goal Setting for Mentoring Session

* **Manager-Participant Meetings**: Each participant meets with their manager to finalize individual and project goals, clarifying what the mentee aims to achieve and how the mentoring program’s outcomes can benefit the project or team. HR can track these goals to ensure they align with both the participant's development and team objectives.  
* **Result**: A documented **Individual Goals Overview** for each participant, forming a foundation for tracking progress on personal and project-related milestones.

## Mentor-Mentee Introduction and Goal Alignment

* **Introductory Meeting**: The mentor and mentee meet to review the participant’s goals and establish common objectives for the session. This meeting is typically driven by the mentee's goals.  
* **Gap Analysis**: The mentor may interview the mentee to identify any knowledge or skill gaps that could inform the mentoring roadmap.  
* **Mentoring Strategy Document**: Together, they draft a **Mentoring Strategy Document**, listing key topics, types of exercises, mentoring style, and a preliminary timeline. The mentor outlines how they plan to help the mentee reach their goals and integrates their own objectives if relevant.  
* **Optional Informal Meeting**: They can also schedule an informal meeting to build a stronger personal connection, fostering trust and open communication.

## Result

### Meetings and Announcements

1. **Onboarding Announcement/Meeting**: Introduce all participants to program logistics and goals.  
2. **Mentor Training Session**: Optional, focused on techniques and tools for effective mentoring.  
3. **Participant-Manager Meeting**: To finalize individual and project goals.  
4. **Mentor-Mentee Introductory Meeting**: To set session goals and discuss strategies.

### Documents

5. **Individual Goals Document**: Outlines each participant’s personal and project-specific goals.  
6. **Mentoring Session Strategy Document**: Provides a high-level roadmap for the session, listing objectives, anticipated challenges, and strategies for addressing them. This document is shared with relevant stakeholders for alignment and transparency.

# Phase 2\. Planning

## Curriculum Development

* **Curriculum Creation**: The mentor takes the high-level mentoring goals and converts them into a **modular curriculum** that breaks down the mentoring process into focused segments, each with:  
  * **Module Goals**: Specific outcomes each module aims to achieve.  
  * **Theoretical Content**: Core materials, readings, or topics that the mentee will review to build foundational knowledge.  
  * **Practice Approaches**: Relevant exercises, projects, or tasks to apply learning practically.  
  * **Feedback Expectations**: Criteria and frequency for feedback to ensure iterative progress.  
* **Detailing Initial Modules**: The mentor develops a detailed plan for the first module(s), setting clear objectives, key concepts, and anticipated outcomes to give both parties a clear starting point.  
* **Iterative Approach**: Allowing room to adjust the curriculum after initial modules lets mentors and mentees adapt as learning progresses, enabling responsive planning based on feedback and evolving goals.

## Project-Based Learning Integration

* **Mentor-Manager Collaboration**: The mentor may meet with the mentee’s manager, or with both the mentor’s and mentee’s managers if applicable, to identify **project tasks** suitable for mentoring purposes. These tasks might be:  
  * Low-priority or low-risk tasks that still contribute to project goals.  
  * Tasks with minimal technical uncertainty but high learning potential, offering hands-on experience without project delays.  
* **Alignment with Project Goals**: Leveraging actual project tasks aligns mentee development with real-world objectives, fostering relevant skills and contributing to ongoing project needs.

## Schedule and Timeline

* **Timeline and Meeting Schedule**: The mentor and mentee meet to set up a timeline, defining a regular schedule for progress check-ins, reviews, and feedback sessions. Meetings might be:  
  * Weekly or biweekly for updates on module progress and feedback.  
  * Monthly or milestone-based to review overall progress and adjust plans as needed.  
* **Calendar Bookings**: Both the mentor and mentee commit to a set schedule by reserving dedicated time in their calendars, ensuring consistency and prioritizing the mentoring relationship.

## Result

### Meetings

1. **Mentor-Managers Meeting**: To identify project tasks that can serve as real-world applications of mentoring goals.  
2. **Mentor-Mentee Planning Meeting**: To finalize the timeline, set up a meeting schedule, and discuss organizational details like meeting formats and preferred check-in methods.

### Documents

3. **Documented Curriculum**: A structured curriculum outlining the goals, learning materials, and practice approach for each module. The document serves as a roadmap, with expectations for each phase to keep the mentor-mentee team on track.  
4. **Detailed First Module Plan**: An actionable plan for the first module, setting the stage for an organized and purposeful start.  
5. **Scheduled Meeting Times**: A documented timeline with booked time in both the mentor’s and mentee’s calendars, ensuring accountability and time commitment.

# Phase 3\. Module Iteration

This phase is **iterative**, focusing on refinement and continuous feedback. Before starting a new iteration, the mentor should ensure that they have a **detailed module plan** that includes theoretical resources, well-defined exercise requirements, and a clear understanding of the expected outcomes.

## Theoretical Unit

* **Module Preparation**: Before beginning a module, the mentor reviews the theoretical materials in detail and identifies key points to focus on. The mentor can think of examples from their experience or prepare questions that will encourage the mentee to apply the theory critically.  
* **Mentee Preparation**: As part of the preparation, the mentee should review the theoretical materials and **prepare questions** to discuss with the mentor. This step encourages active participation and fosters deeper understanding.  
* **Discussion**: Mentor and mentee meet to discuss the theoretical material, with the mentee actively participating by asking questions and engaging in discussions about how the theory can be applied in practical contexts.

## Practical Unit

* **Material and Content Review**: As part of the module detailing process, the mentor reviews and verifies the practical materials and tasks that the mentee will work on. The mentor sets up clear requirements and defines what constitutes "done" for each task.  
* **Exercise Discussion**: The mentor and mentee meet to go over the **exercise requirements**, ensuring the mentee understands what is expected and has a plan for tackling the task.  
* **Execution and Progress Review**: The mentee begins working on the task. The mentor and mentee meet regularly according to their timeline to review progress, address any challenges, and provide feedback.  
* **Next Module Planning**: While the mentee works on the current task, the mentor begins planning the next module to ensure continuity and momentum.

## Review Unit

* **Module Review Meeting**: Once the task is completed, the mentor and mentee meet to review the results of the module. The mentor evaluates the mentee’s performance according to the goals and expectations set for the module.  
* **Feedback Exchange**: Both the mentor and mentee give and receive **feedback** on the module. This includes discussing what went well, what could be improved, and any areas of confusion or missed opportunities for learning.  
* **Documentation**: Both mentor and mentee separately document their achievements and the feedback received, sharing these with relevant stakeholders (e.g., managers, HR).  
* **Optional Review with Stakeholders**: Mentors, mentees, and potentially their managers or HR meet to discuss the overall feedback and progress. This provides an opportunity for alignment and further guidance on next steps.  
* **Mentor Sync Session (Optional)**: Mentors can also meet with each other in sync sessions to share progress, challenges, and solutions. This allows for a collaborative environment and helps ensure consistency across different mentoring relationships.

## Result

1. **Completed Module**: The module plan has been executed, with mentee progress documented according to the set goals and expectations.  
2. **Feedback and Achievements**: Mentor and mentee share their individual feedback and documented achievements with stakeholders, ensuring alignment and transparency.  
3. **Next Module Plan**: The mentor has prepared the details for the next module, ensuring that the mentoring process remains forward-thinking and adaptable.

# Phase 4\. Evaluation

In this phase, the focus is on assessing the outcomes of the mentoring session, capturing feedback, and planning for the future.

## Review and Feedback

* **Mentor-Mentee Evaluation Meeting**: The mentor and mentee meet to:  
  * Summarize the **results and achievements** of the mentoring session.  
  * Share **overall feedback** on the mentoring experience, discussing what worked well and areas for improvement.  
  * Identify **plans for next sessions** (if applicable), outlining future goals or additional areas for development.  
* **Manager-Participant Feedback Meeting**: The participant and their manager meet to compare the **mentoring goals** set at the beginning of the session with the **actual results** achieved. This meeting helps ensure alignment between individual development and project needs.  
* **Program Review with HR**: HR, along with managers, reviews the overall effectiveness of the mentoring session. This involves comparing the **mentoring session goals** with actual outcomes, identifying any gaps, successes, and opportunities for improvement in the program itself.

## Recognition

* **Mentor Micro-Conference**: Mentors meet to share **approaches**, lessons learned, and best practices. This peer-sharing session allows mentors to learn from each other’s experiences and refine their mentoring techniques.  
* **Company Recognition**: Acknowledging and celebrating the achievements of both mentors and mentees, whether through formal recognition, certificates, or public acknowledgment at company events. This recognition boosts morale and highlights the value of mentoring within the company.

## Result

1. **Program Feedback**: A detailed evaluation of the mentoring program's outcomes, identifying what worked, areas for improvement, and next steps.  
2. **Recognition of Achievements**: Mentors and mentees are recognized for their efforts and contributions to the program, fostering a culture of continuous learning and growth.
