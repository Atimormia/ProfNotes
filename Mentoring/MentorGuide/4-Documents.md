# Mentor Guide 04: Document Templates and Content Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines the content of every written artifact the Mentoring Program and Guides 01 through 03 require. Its role is to answer two questions for each artifact: what decision or evidence must survive as a written record, and what the minimal acceptable content is. It does not provide rigid fill-in forms. Depth should scale with the module or session it documents; a two-week Reactive exercise and a six-month curriculum area do not warrant the same level of detail.

## Why These Records Exist

A record that only exists in conversation cannot be compared against later, cannot be handed to a Season Synthesis, and cannot be picked up by a different Mentor if the pairing changes. Every artifact in this guide exists to make a decision, an expectation, or a piece of evidence outlive the conversation that produced it, not to satisfy a reporting requirement for its own sake.

## A General Principle: Separate Before Reconciled

Where an artifact is produced by both the Mentor and the Mentee, each party writes their portion independently first, before any joint discussion. Writing separately prevents one party's framing from anchoring the other's, particularly where the Mentee's own honest sense of their readiness matters and should not be shaped in advance by what they expect the Mentor to say. This applies specifically to the Module Outcome Record below.

## Access and Revision

Two rules apply across every artifact in this guide, since neither follows automatically from the templates themselves.

**State access before content is written, not after.** A confidentiality rule announced only after a document exists does not produce honest content; it only explains, retroactively, why some content was withheld. Each artifact below states who can read it and when. Where a rule isn't stated explicitly for a given artifact, the default is: visible to Mentor and Mentee, and to any stakeholder named in that artifact's own description, nobody else, unless the Mentee agrees otherwise.

**Revise with a dated note, don't silently overwrite.** Any material change to a stated goal, a module's closure definition, or a gap classification should carry a short, dated line stating what changed and why, alongside the update itself. This is not a full version history requirement, one line is sufficient, but a plain overwrite with no trace of the earlier version leaves a later Season Synthesis, or a new Mentor picking up the pairing, unable to tell whether a goal was met, revised for a good reason, or quietly abandoned.

## 1. Individual Goals and Baseline Document

**Exists to preserve:** the Mentee's stated goals and their diagnosed starting point (Guide 01's Baseline), together, so later progress has a fixed reference.

**Author and Timing:** the Mentee, with their Manager, for the goals portion; the Mentor, for the Baseline portion, following the Diagnostic Toolkit's method. Produced once, at the start of a mentoring relationship or a new curriculum area.

**Access:** visible to Mentor, Mentee, and the Mentee's Manager. Any revision to a stated goal carries a dated note per the Access and Revision rules above.

**Minimal Content:**
* Personal and project-specific goals, as discussed with the Manager
* Baseline evidence sources used (self-report, peer input, work archaeology, task, per Guide 01)
* Initial gap classification (knowledge, practice, or judgment) and rough urgency
* Agreed definition of what closing each identified gap will look like

**Feeds Forward:** every later Checkpoint compares against this document; the Mentoring Strategy Document is built from it.

---

## 2. Mentoring Strategy Document

**Exists to preserve:** the session-level roadmap connecting the Mentee's goals to a plan for reaching them, before any specific module is designed.

**Author and Timing:** Mentor and Mentee jointly, following the introductory goal-alignment meeting. Produced once per session, revisited if goals shift materially.

**Access:** visible to Mentor and Mentee. Shared with the Manager and HR for alignment purposes, per the original Program's stated practice.

**Minimal Content:**
* Key topics or gap areas the session intends to cover
* Anticipated exercise types and mentoring style
* Preliminary timeline
* Anticipated challenges and how they might be addressed

**Feeds Forward:** the Curriculum Record is developed within the boundaries this document sets. If Guide 01's Season Synthesis later shows a goal was miscalibrated, this document is the one revised, with a dated note per the Access and Revision rules.

---

## 3. Curriculum Record

**Exists to preserve:** the running, module-by-module breakdown of the session, and the Step 0 through Step 3 decisions (Guide 02) made for each module as it is added.

**Author and Timing:** the Mentor, updated as each module is planned, not written in full up front. The first module or two may be detailed at Phase 2; later modules are added iteratively as the session progresses.

**Access:** visible to Mentor and Mentee throughout. Each added or revised module entry carries a dated note, since this document changes more frequently than most others in this guide.

**Minimal Content, per module entry:**
* Module goal and the gap it addresses (from the Individual Goals and Baseline Document or a later Checkpoint)
* Material source: authored or curated, tracked separately for theory and exercise (Guide 02, Step 0)
* Design mode selected and why (Guide 02, Step 1)
* Exercise format and task source selected and why (Guide 02, Steps 2 and 3)

**Feeds Forward:** each entry becomes the basis for that module's Module Plan; the full record is what a Season Synthesis or a future Mentor reviews to understand how the curriculum evolved.

---

## 4. Module Plan

**Exists to preserve:** the specific, actionable detail for one module before it starts, including the closure definition required by every toolkit's hard rule.

**Author and Timing:** the Mentor, reviewed with the Mentee before the module begins. Produced fresh for each module.

**Access:** visible to Mentor and Mentee. Not routinely shared beyond the pairing unless referenced in the Curriculum Record's summary or a stakeholder review.

**Minimal Content:**
* Objectives and key concepts specific to this module
* Theoretical material to be reviewed, and by whom (Mentor prepares examples and questions; Mentee prepares questions, per the original Program's Theoretical Unit)
* Exercise requirements and Definition of Done
* Agreed closure definition (what evidence will confirm the gap has closed, per Guide 01's hard rule; for Planned Mode, this includes the required transfer step per Guide 02, Step 1)
* Meeting cadence for this module specifically, where it differs from the session default

**Feeds Forward:** the Module Outcome Record is assessed directly against this document's closure definition. If the closure definition changes after the module has started, this counts as a material revision under the Access and Revision rules above.

---

## 5. Module Outcome Record

**Exists to preserve:** the Checkpoint evidence (Guide 01), the Exit Evidence for any Guide 03 stage transition attempted during the module, and both parties' independent read on how it went.

**Author and Timing:** Mentor and Mentee, **written separately first**, then reconciled in the Module Review Meeting. Produced once per module, at its conclusion or clear stall point.

**Access:** the Mentor's independent draft and the Mentee's independent draft are visible only to each other until reconciliation. This is stated to the Mentee before they write, not after, since a confidentiality rule announced retroactively does not produce honest content. Only the reconciled summary carries forward to the Season Summary or to any stakeholder beyond the pairing; the independent drafts themselves are not shared further without the Mentee's agreement, even after reconciliation.

**Minimal Content:**
* Checkpoint evidence gathered, by source (Guide 01, Step 2 of the Diagnostic Method)
* Comparison against the Baseline and against this module's closure definition
* Where applicable, Exit Evidence supporting or not yet supporting a Guide 03 stage transition
* Mentor's independent feedback (written before reconciliation)
* Mentee's independent feedback (written before reconciliation)
* Reconciled summary, following joint discussion
* Next module's planning notes, if work has already begun

**Feeds Forward:** the season's set of reconciled summaries is what Guide 01's Season Synthesis reads as a trajectory. Where a Guide 03 stage transition is confirmed, note it here and carry it into the Individual Goals and Baseline Document's running record for that area of responsibility.

---

## 6. Season Summary

**Exists to preserve:** the Season Synthesis reading (Guide 01) and the session-level reflection described in the original Program's Phase 4, together.

**Author and Timing:** Mentor and Mentee jointly, at the end of a session. Produced once per season.

**Access:** visible to Mentor and Mentee; shared with the Manager and HR as the primary season-level artifact they receive, so that neither needs access to the Module Outcome Records' independent drafts to understand how the season went.

**Minimal Content:**
* Season Synthesis: the pattern read across the season's Module Outcome Records, not a re-run of any single Checkpoint
* Where the synthesis revises an earlier gap classification or urgency estimate
* Results and achievements against the original Individual Goals and Baseline Document
* What worked, what didn't, and why
* Plans for the next session, if applicable

**Feeds Forward:** the Manager Comparison Record and the HR Program Review both draw on this document rather than reconstructing their own view of the season independently.

---

## 7. Manager Comparison Record

**Exists to preserve:** whether the mentoring goals set at the start of a session were met, from the perspective of the Mentee's Manager, who has a stake in the outcome distinct from the Mentor's.

**Author and Timing:** the Manager and Mentee jointly, following the Season Summary. Produced once per season.

**Access:** visible to Manager, Mentee, and HR. Not routinely shared with the Mentor unless the Mentee agrees, since the Mentor's own view is already captured in the Season Summary.

**Minimal Content:**
* Original goals as stated in the Individual Goals and Baseline Document
* Actual results, per the Season Summary
* Manager's assessment of alignment with team or project needs
* Any adjustment to the Mentee's development plan going forward

**Feeds Forward:** informs the Mentee's broader development record outside the mentoring relationship itself; where a Guide 03 Own It transition occurred during the season, this is also the appropriate place to formally surface it to the Manager, per Guide 03's visibility note.

---

## 8. HR Program Review

**Exists to preserve:** whether the *program*, not any individual Mentee, is functioning as intended. This is a distinct subject from every artifact above.

**Author and Timing:** HR, with input from Managers, at the season or year boundary. Produced at a program level, aggregating across multiple mentoring pairs, not per pairing.

**Access:** visible to HR and Managers. Presented to Mentors in aggregated, non-attributable form (per-pairing detail is not circulated program-wide), consistent with the Access and Revision rules' default of not exposing pairing-specific content beyond those named in it.

**Minimal Content:**
* Aggregate comparison of session goals to outcomes across pairs
* Patterns worth the program's attention: gaps, successes, recurring friction (see Guide 08 for when a specific pairing's friction should feed into this review)
* Library health: which modules were promoted from Guide 02's authored-or-curated tracking, and evidence they generalize across more than one Mentee
* Recommendations for the next session period

**Feeds Forward:** informs whether and how the program's structure, cadence, or platform (per the original Program's setup phase) should change going into the next session.

---

## Result

A consolidated set of eight artifacts, six mentee-facing and two stakeholder-facing, each with a defined author, timing, access rule, and minimal content, together with a general revision rule preventing silent overwrites. This closes the two structural gaps most likely to erode trust in the document set over time: unstated access rules discouraging honest writing, and undocumented revisions making a pairing's history untraceable to anyone who joins it later.
